Why the divisor matters

Imagine a 2-for-1 split: a $100 share becomes two $50 shares. The company’s value is unchanged. In a cap-weighted index, price × shares stays the same. In a price-weighted index, the price sum falls, so the divisor must change to avoid a false drop. Actual treatments depend on the index methodology.

From calculation to system design

A reproducible result needs a versioned snapshot: prices, eligible shares, constituents, currency rates, divisor and methodology. Define stale prices, rounding, market hours and whether the index includes reinvested dividends.

  • Ingest: validate source events, track sequence numbers, detect gaps and replay or reconcile according to the feed contract.
  • Calculate: update the affected contribution and reconcile against a full calculation. Apply corporate actions at their defined effective time.
  • Commit and publish: persist the accepted input position, calculation state and an outbox record atomically when they share a database. Relay at least once; consumers deduplicate outputs.
  • Recover: replay durable inputs with versioned reference data. Define how late ticks and corrections affect published history, and fence competing leaders.

Remember: price or eligible market value determines influence; the divisor preserves continuity; versioned inputs make a result reproducible.
